# nearlysans
A typeface based on FreeSans, but with distinguishable l and I. May be useful in scientific documents and graphics.

The typeface NearlySans, consisting of four fonts (regular, bold, oblique, and bold oblique), is a modified version of the FreeSans typeface of 2012-05-03. It includes all glyphs of FreeSans, and three additional ones (see below).

The main modifications are the addition of a hook to "l" and serifs to "I", in order to make fonts that are similar to Arial and Helvetica but with readily distinguishable "l", "I", and "1".

New glyphs: Horizontal Line Extension (U+23AF), Long Leftward Arrow (U+27F5), and Long Rightward Arrow (U+27F6).
These can be combined, so that you can create unbroken arrows of various lengths (e.g., for chemical equations).

Other modifications:
(a) New versions of the Greek lower-case letter nu (U+03BD) are included, so that they are no longer copies of the Latin letter v.
(b) The rounded parts of Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic characters have been compressed a bit vertically, that is, their overshoots have been reduced. As a result, rounded characters (such as O, Q, c, and e) and non-rounded characters (such as F, M, x, and z) are now closer in height. This is designed to make NearlySans appear more visually uniform, and more similar to other free sans-serif fonts.

Additional details of the modifications made are in the Changelog file.

Additional information about this typeface's parent, FreeSans, is on the GNU Freefont website (see link below).
